Separatrix calibration demo

Two ways an entropy-driven refinement loop can be blind on the Newton fractal f(z) = z³−1 of arXiv:2608.14743, measured on the paper's own benchmark: buried islands that the acquisition never reaches, and peninsulas that are reached but too thin to resolve. Every number here comes from results/ in the repository; the fractal and the sampling grids are computed live in your browser.

Basins of attraction of z³ − 1

Scroll to zoom · drag to pan · click an island to select it. The three colours are the three roots; the white-ish filaments are points that take many Newton steps — the fractal separatrix.

A uniform grid almost never lands on an island

68 small patches of one basin are completely enclosed by another. They are 3.0–6.3 fine cells across at 3200², i.e. 0.0028–0.0059 units, against a window of 3 units — and they cover 0.0088 % of the plane.

The acquisition step of the loop keeps the top 10 % of the evaluation grid by entropy and jitters those points. Its support is the training grid, so an island that the grid missed can never be proposed, however large its entropy. Measured: 92 % of the buried islands are already above the 85th entropy percentile in round 1.

Recovery vs samples across the island

Peninsulas: recovery vs corridor thickness

Thin corridors (t/h ≲ 2) are frozen into the neighbouring basin. More samples barely help: the root is already densely sampled.

Only the root survives. One bin further out the classifier is at chance and the neighbouring basin takes 31–40 % of the cells.

What the paper's own protocol sees

40 000 uniform initial conditions (200 × 200), evaluation on 160 000 points (400 × 400), the top 10 % by entropy (16 000) used to train the generative model, scored by Chamfer / sliced Wasserstein between that set and the model's samples.

Same models, two populations

Why the sign flips

The refinement loop scores "separatrix accuracy" on the current training set. That set is re-drawn every round and becomes boundary-dominated (0 % → 55 % of the points), so the round-to-round difference mixes a model change with a population change.

Isolated with one oracle held fixed (experiments/metric_bias.py): the escape-proxy accuracy falls by 0.047 on the changing training set and rises by 0.074 on a fixed uniform population — the same five models, the same oracle.

The consequence for this repository: the "oversampling damages the model" reading was a measurement artefact. What actually happens is that accuracy inside the band improves (0.467 → 0.533) and the calibration error inside the band falls (0.083 → 0.060).
